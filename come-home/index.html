01

What Elder Gilbert actually said

His first address since being ordained to the Quorum of the Twelve, February 2026.
He chose two names to witness of

Redeemer

He buys us back. The price is already paid.

Repairer

“When we fall short, He repairs the breaches in our lives.”
A builder's word, not a rescue word. Something broken is being rebuilt — not merely retrieved.
“Come Home,” ¶3
Where the name comes from
Isaiah 58:12

12And they that shall be of thee shall build the old waste places: thou shalt raise up the foundations of many generations; and thou shalt be called, The repairer of the breach, The restorer of paths to dwell in.

Read it aloud. Notice the verbs: build, raise up, restore. And notice who does the building — “they that shall be of thee.”
“
Because of our covenant with God, He will never tire in His efforts to help us, and we will never exhaust His merciful patience with us.
And “should [we] stray, He will help [us] find [our] way back.”
President Russell M. Nelson · “Come Home,” ¶10
Elder Gilbert named four reasons people don't come home

And he gave a real person for every one of them.

“I don't belong.”
Tammy — out of the Church 30 years
“I don't measure up.”
Luis — didn't think he was worthy of baptism
“I have doubts.”
Justin & Kenna — moved away to leave the faith
“My traditions won't let me.”
John — held back by his late parents' faith
BARRIER ONE

“I don't belong here anymore.”

Tammy had been away thirty years. What moved her was a temple open house and a bishop who kept encouraging her. She later received her endowment.
Worth noticing: she was welcomed before she was all the way back. Belonging came first. It was not the reward for returning — it was the road.
BARRIER TWO

“I don't measure up.”

You don't have to be perfect to be in this Church. You just have to do your best, and Christ will make up the difference.
Elder Gilbert, to Luis — on the steps of the San Antonio Temple
He was baptized a month later. His family was sealed in that same temple.
BARRIER THREE

“I have doubts.”

Justin and Kenna moved away specifically to leave the faith behind. The door reopened because their eight-year-old son wanted to be baptized.
Elder Gilbert's counsel was not to resolve every doubt. It was to anchor in what they still believed and let the Lord work on the rest. They were sealed as a family.
BARRIER FOUR

“My traditions won't let me.”

John, a former BYU football player, turned it down out of loyalty to his deceased parents' faith.
He joined once he realized his parents would have supported him. The obstacle was never doctrine. It was love pointed the wrong direction — and it took someone patient enough to stay near while he worked it out.
Look at that list again

Four barriers. Not one of them is
“they don't know the Church is true.”

Every barrier he named is a feeling — unwelcome, unworthy, unsure, unfree.

Information does not move any of those.
Relationship does.

The thing all four stories share

There was always a person there.

A bishop. A missionary. An eight-year-old boy. A teammate.

Nobody in that talk came home alone.

02

The gift, not the program

Why we lead with Christ and His Atonement — and not with the ward.
The hinge of this whole discussion

Elder Gilbert says only one thing fully restores light and joy.

It isn't the ward. It isn't the activities, the standards, or the community. Those are good — but they are not the gift.

So our invitations should sound like the gift.

Two ways to invite the same person

One asks them to join. One offers them relief.

Inviting to the institution

“Come to church with us.”

“We have a really great ward.”

“There's an activity Thursday.”

Inviting to the gift

“There's something that carried me through the worst year of my life.”

“You don't have to be fixed first.”

“I know Someone who knows exactly what this feels like.”

Neither column is dishonest. But only one of them answers the four barriers Elder Gilbert just named.
1 Nephi 8:11–12

11And it came to pass that I did go forth and partake of the fruit thereof; and I beheld that it was most sweet, above all that I ever before tasted. Yea, and I beheld that the fruit thereof was white, to exceed all the whiteness that I had ever seen.

12And as I partook of the fruit thereof it filled my soul with exceedingly great joy; wherefore, I began to be desirous that my family should partake of it also; for I knew that it was desirable above all other fruit.

Lehi tastes it — and his very next breath is wanting his family to have it. Missionary work is the reflex of someone who has tasted.
03

Hunter's story

Hunter· 5–10 minutes
Hunter didn't come back — he came in. Two of Elder Gilbert's four stories were the same: Luis and John were never members either.
Everything we've said so far, in one life

What Hunter will walk us through

  1. What it's actually like out there.Living outside Utah — and outside the Church — pulls on you in ways most of this room has never had to feel. The drinking. The drugs. The drift.
  2. Where that road goes.At a minimum, someone who is lost. At worst, someone who doesn't make it out at all.
  3. What it takes to get out.Seeing the damage clearly — and people who accept you before you've changed anything.
  4. What you can see from the other side.The flow state. Looking back and finally understanding how blessed you were, in every part of your life.

He was accepted
before he ever believed.

And through that acceptance he saw two things at once — that he was loved, and that there was a better way to live.
Patience, service, and love from others — told from the inside, by someone who came in from the outside.

Elk Ridge 7th Ward Mission Plan

Bring Them Hither

Jeff· about 12 minutes
Where the name comes from
3 Nephi 17:7, 9

7Have ye any that are sick among you? Bring them hither. Have ye any that are lame, or blind, or halt, or maimed, or leprous, or that are withered, or that are deaf, or that are afflicted in any manner? Bring them hither and I will heal them, for I have compassion upon you; my bowels are filled with mercy.

9And it came to pass that when he had thus spoken, all the multitude, with one accord, did go forth with their sick and their afflicted, and their lame, and with their blind, and with their dumb, and with all them that were afflicted in any manner; and he did heal them every one as they were brought forth unto him.

He did not walk the crowd and find them one by one. He asked the people who already knew where the afflicted were to bring them. The healing was always His. The bringing was theirs.
The invitation, in three words

Love · Share · Invite

  1. Love.Build genuine friendships. Minister with Christlike compassion — not as a step toward something else.
  2. Share.Bear testimony of Christ and the restored gospel naturally, in the course of an ordinary conversation.
  3. Invite.Extend simple invitations — to worship, to serve, to follow the Savior.
PILLAR ONE

Home and neighborhood connection

  • Visit every household inside the ward boundary
  • Each visit carries a brief, non-preachy message centered on Christ

The goal: neighbors feel surrounded by people who love them — not by recruiters.

PILLAR TWO

Teach the gospel, support the missionaries

  • Study and apply Preach My Gospel — the second edition is written for every member, not just full-time missionaries
  • Coordinate with the full-time missionaries: fed, supported, connected to the ward
  • Spotlight one ward missionary each month in the newsletter
PILLAR THREE

The YSA and the rising generation

  • A dedicated ward missionary assigned to minister to and support the youth
  • Casual group activities — pickleball, dinners — as natural on-ramps
  • Invite them individually and by name. Don't assume a YSA will show up to a “family” event
  • Give grandparents and parents of struggling youth tools and connection
A useful test for any plan

Which part of the plan reaches each of Elder Gilbert's four people?

Who feels they don't belong
Who feels they don't measure up
Who doubts
Who is held by tradition
If one of them has no answer in the plan, that's not a failure — it's the most useful thing we could name out loud today.
Which is why the plan is only half of it

A ward mission plan isn't a sales plan.

It's a plan for creating more moments where the Spirit can do the teaching.

Second half of Jeff's time

Now — leading with the Spirit

The plan is the what. This is the how — and without it, a plan is only logistics.
He asks the question, then answers it Himself
Doctrine and Covenants 50:13–14

13Wherefore, I the Lord ask you this question — unto what were ye ordained?

14To preach my gospel by the Spirit, even the Comforter which was sent forth to teach the truth.

He asks the question and then answers it Himself. We were not ordained to teach the truth. We were ordained to preach by the Spirit — who teaches the truth.
2 Nephi 33:1

1And now I, Nephi, cannot write all the things which were taught among my people; neither am I mighty in writing, like unto speaking; for when a man speaketh by the power of the Holy Ghost the power of the Holy Ghost carrieth it unto the hearts of the children of men.

Unto the heart — not into it. The Spirit carries it to the door. The person still opens it. That is Elder Gilbert's line: “In the end, everyone must make their own choices to come home.”
Which leaves one question

If the Spirit is the teacher — what is our actual job?

To build the conditions where He can be felt. The patience. The service. The love. The relationship.

We set the table. He teaches.

Before we close

Who have you been thinking about during this lesson?

You don't have to say the name out loud.

What would showing them more love look like this week?
