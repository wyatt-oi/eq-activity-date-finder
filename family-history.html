Making the ConnectionUsing today's tools to connect with the people who made us.

This is the ward's family history plan — why we're doing it and the one thing we're asking of you. It's short on purpose. Dates and activities live on the calendar.

Why we're doing this

Rusty and the Bishop have already named the goal: baptize a ward in heaven — at least one complete set of ordinances for the family of every member of this ward.

But nobody starts there. People start with a story — a grandpa, a photo, a voice they don't want to lose. So that's where we're starting too. Connection first; ordinances follow.

The one thing we're asking

Pick one facet. That's it.

Family history isn't one thing. It's temple work, indexing, the family tree, recorded stories, photos and video, pulling a name with Ordinances Ready. Nobody has to do all of it. Our whole job this year is helping you find the one piece that lights you up, and helping you start.

There is no wrong way to do family history, and nobody gets corrected here for doing it “improperly.”

Recorded storiesSit down with a parent or grandparent. Their voice, their face, in their words.
Photos & videoGet the shoebox onto FamilySearch Memories before it's lost.
The family treeFill in what you know. Fix what's wrong. Add sources.
Temple workTake your own family's names to the temple.
Ordinances ReadyTwo taps in the app and you have a name to take.
IndexingMake records searchable for everyone else. Do it in your slippers.

Every month, without adding a meeting

  • Relief Society temple night — already happening, every month. This is the engine. Elders quorum promotes it and comes along.
  • Fast Sunday — the deacons deliver the invite for whatever's next, with a short word from the presidencies. If you want to know what's coming, that invite is it.
  • Once a quarter — one member shares a three-to-five minute family history experience in quorum or class. Clint volunteered to go first.

How you actually get started

Gatherings open doors. Visits are where things change.

  • Say yes at any gathering, or just tell someone. Rusty and the consultants visit interested members in twos and threes.
  • Ward council members carry the two-minute message below and pass warm leads to Rusty. Nobody freelances a full lesson on a doorstep — spark the interest, hand it off.
  • One visit does one thing: help you start your one facet. Nothing more.

Through the year there are a handful of gatherings built around this — a kickoff, a car show, a Dutch oven night, a temple celebration, and quarterly hands-on nights where you bring a phone and leave having actually recorded or uploaded one thing. You don't need to track any of that here. Every date is on the calendar, checked against ball games, school breaks and the stake calendar.

The two-minute message

If you carry one thing from this page, carry this.

“Can I tell you the thing that got me? Someone pointed out that if you haven't sat down and recorded your parents or grandparents — their voice, their face, their stories — that's the window that closes. And with the tools we have now, a recorded story lasts forever; your great-grandkids could hear Grandpa tell it himself.

The ward's doing something simple this year: everybody picks one small piece of family history — stories, photos, the tree, indexing, a temple name — just one. Which one would you pick? … Rusty and his consultants will come help you start. Can I have him reach out?”

What success looks like

We can't make anyone love family history, and we're not going to try. Success is simpler than that: we offered, we inspired, we invited — on a calendar, all year, without burying anybody.

If Rusty can end every month saying “here's what's next and here's who we invited,” this worked. Attendance is a bonus. Ordinances are the fruit that follows.

The budget, for the record, is close to nothing by design — potluck and Dutch oven, donated pie, a car show that costs nothing, and a temple night that already exists.
