Why we're doing this

The goal is a ward baptized in heaven — at least one complete set of ordinances for the family of every member of this ward. That's what this whole year is pointed at, and it's the ward's aim, not any one person's assignment.

Some of us are ready to work straight from the tree, and if that's you, go. Plenty of others start somewhere else entirely — a story. A grandpa, a photo, a voice you don't want to lose. Both are real family history, and both end up in the same place. Connection and ordinances feed each other; you can start from whichever end is actually pulling at you.

The one thing we're asking

Pick one facet. That's it.

Family history isn't one thing. It's temple work, indexing, the family tree, recorded stories, photos and video, pulling a name with Ordinances Ready. Nobody has to do all of it. Our whole job this year is helping you find the one piece that lights you up, and helping you start.

There is no wrong way to do family history, and nobody gets corrected here for doing it “improperly.”

Every month, without adding a meeting

  • The ward temple night — already happening, every month. This is the engine, and it is the whole ward's, not one auxiliary's. Come whether or not you have a name of your own yet.
  • Fast Sunday — the deacons deliver the invite for whatever's next, with a short word from the presidencies. If you want to know what's coming, that invite is it.
  • Once a quarter — one member shares a three-to-five minute family history experience in quorum or class. Someone has already volunteered to go first.

How you actually get started

Gatherings open doors. Visits are where things change.

  • Say yes at any gathering, or just tell someone. The ward temple and family history leader and the consultants visit interested members in twos and threes.
  • Ward council members carry the two-minute message below and pass warm leads on. Nobody freelances a full lesson on a doorstep — spark the interest, hand it off.
  • One visit does one thing: help you start your one facet. Nothing more.

Through the year there are a handful of gatherings built around this — a kickoff, a car show, a Dutch oven night, a temple celebration, and quarterly hands-on nights where you bring a phone and leave having actually recorded or uploaded one thing. They're all listed below.

The year, in order

The year has two halves on purpose. August to November we're finding — names, stories, photos, whatever you've got. January to May we're taking them to the temple. Find first, then go. You can't do the second half without the first.

The two-minute message

If you carry one thing from this page, carry this.

“Can I tell you the thing that got me? Someone pointed out that if you haven't sat down and recorded your parents or grandparents — their voice, their face, their stories — that's the window that closes. And with the tools we have now, a recorded story lasts forever; your great-grandkids could hear Grandpa tell it himself.

The ward's doing something simple this year: everybody picks one small piece of family history — stories, photos, the tree, indexing, a temple name — just one. Which one would you pick? … Our temple and family history leader and his consultants will come help you start. Can I have them reach out?”

What success looks like

We can't make anyone love family history, and we're not going to try. Success is simpler than that: we offered, we inspired, we invited — on a calendar, all year, without burying anybody.

If we can end every month saying “here's what's next and here's who we invited,” this worked. Attendance is a bonus. Ordinances are the fruit that follows.

The budget, for the record, is close to nothing by design — potluck and Dutch oven, donated pie, a car show that costs nothing, and a temple night that already exists.
